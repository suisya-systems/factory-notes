# Request: a workshop's stock, customer orders with backorders, project tasks that use parts, a lending library, and customers' shared expenses

An HTTP API for a small workshop that sells parts to customers, uses the same parts in the tasks of its own projects, lends books to its customers, and keeps the shared expenses of groups of customers. Every request and response body is JSON. In a request body, a field written with `?` may be left out (its default is given); every other field named is required, and leaving it out is 400. Ids are strings the server assigns. Quantities and prices are integers; prices are in yen. Dates are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings; a date that is not a real calendar date is 400. Durations are whole calendar days.

## Stock and orders

- `POST /products` with `{"sku": string, "name": string, "price": integer}` registers a product. `sku` is 1 to 20 characters of `A`-`Z`, `0`-`9` and `-`, unique; `name` is 1 to 100 characters; `price` is 1 to 1,000,000. 201 with the product. A product is `{"sku", "name", "price", "free", "allocated", "backordered"}`; a new product has 0 of each. `PUT /products/{sku}/price` with `{"price"}` changes the price for orders placed afterwards only (200, the product).
- `POST /customers` with `{"name", "creditLimit"}` registers a customer (`name` 1 to 100 characters, `creditLimit` 0 to 100,000,000). 201 with the customer (see the library). A customer's `openValue` is the sum of `quantity * unitPrice` over the lines of their orders that are neither shipped nor cancelled, plus their unpaid library `fines`. Task parts never count in it.
- `POST /orders` with `{"customerId", "lines": [{"sku", "quantity"}, ...]}` places an order: 1 to 20 lines, each sku at most once, `quantity` 1 to 1,000. Each line's `unitPrice` is the product's price at this moment. A blocked customer (see the library) is refused with 409. If the customer's `openValue` plus this order's total would exceed `creditLimit`, the order is refused with 409 and nothing changes. Otherwise each line, in the order given, takes as many units as it can from the product's `free` units; the rest of the line is backordered. 201 with the order.
- An order is `{"id", "customerId", "status", "lines": [{"sku", "quantity", "unitPrice", "allocated"}, ...], "total"}`, lines in the order given, `total` the sum of `quantity * unitPrice`. `status` is `cancelled` or `shipped` once that happened; otherwise `allocated` when every line is fully allocated, `backordered` when no unit of any line is allocated, and `partial` in between.
- **Part lines.** A task's parts (below) are lines too: each part line has a `quantity` and an `allocated` count and takes units exactly as an order line does. A task takes units when it is added, in the order its parts are given.
- For a product, `allocated` is the number of its units allocated to lines of orders not yet shipped or cancelled and to part lines of tasks not yet done or removed, and `backordered` is the number of units those lines still miss (`quantity` minus `allocated`, summed).
- **Where units go.** Whenever units of a product become available (a receipt, a cancellation, a reduced line, a return, a removed task), they go first to that product's backordered lines, order lines and part lines alike, oldest first (the order placed or the task added earliest, or taking units again; within one order or task the line given first), each line filled as far as possible; units left over become `free`. So a product never has `free` units while it has backordered lines.
- `POST /receipts` with `{"sku", "quantity"}` (1 to 10,000) records units arriving. 201 with `{"sku", "quantity", "allocated": [{"orderId" or "taskId", "quantity"}, ...], "toFree"}`: the units that went to each order or task in the order they were given, then the rest.
- `POST /orders/{id}/ship` ships an order whose status is `allocated` (409 otherwise; also 409 while its customer has an overdue loan: today is after its `dueOn`): its allocated units leave the shop. 200 with the order.
- `POST /orders/{id}/cancel` cancels an order that is not shipped or cancelled (409 otherwise). Its allocated units become available (see above) product by product, in the order of the order's lines. 200 with the order.
- `PATCH /orders/{id}/lines/{sku}` with `{"quantity"}` (1 to 1,000) changes a line of an order that is not shipped or cancelled (409 otherwise; 404 if the order has no such line). Raising it is refused with 409 when the customer is blocked, or when the customer's `openValue` plus the increase times the line's `unitPrice` would exceed `creditLimit`; the line keeps its `unitPrice` and its place among the backorders; the extra units are taken from `free` as far as possible. Lowering it below `allocated` makes the surplus allocated units available. 200 with the order.
- `POST /orders/{id}/returns` with `{"sku", "quantity"}` takes back units of a shipped order: at most the line's quantity minus the units of that line already returned (409 if more; 409 if the order is not shipped). The units become available. 201 with `{"orderId", "sku", "quantity"}`. Returns do not change the order's status or its customer's `openValue`; a return of an order named by an expense also refunds that expense (see shared expenses).
- `GET /products/{sku}`, `GET /orders/{id}` and `GET /customers/{id}` return 200 with the product, order or customer as defined above. `GET /products/{sku}/backorders` returns 200 with `[{"orderId" or "taskId", "missing"}, ...]`, the backordered lines of that product in the order they will be filled.

## Projects and tasks

- `POST /projects` with `{"name": string, "startDate": date}` creates a project (name 1 to 100 characters). 201 with `{"id", "name", "startDate"}`.
- `POST /projects/{id}/tasks` with `{"title": string, "duration": integer, "dependsOn"?: [taskId, ...], "parts"?: [{"sku", "quantity"}, ...]}` adds a task (title 1 to 100 characters, `duration` 1 to 365, `dependsOn` defaults to `[]`, each id a task of the same project, listed at most once; 404 for an unknown id; `parts` defaults to `[]`, at most 20, each sku a product (404 otherwise) listed at most once, `quantity` 1 to 1,000). At most 100 tasks per project. 201 with the task. A task is `{"id", "title", "duration", "dependsOn", "parts": [{"sku", "quantity", "allocated"}, ...], "status", "startedOn", "finishedOn"}`; `dependsOn` is kept in the order given, then the order added. A task's **dependents** are the tasks whose `dependsOn` contains it.
- `POST /tasks/{id}/dependencies` with `{"on": taskId}` makes the task depend on another task of the same project. 409 if it already does, if `on` is the task itself, or if the new dependency would make a cycle (the task would come to depend on itself through other tasks). Adding a dependency on a task that is not `done` to a task that is `doing` is also 409. 200 with the task. `DELETE /tasks/{id}/dependencies/{onId}` removes one (404 if the task has no such dependency); 200 with the task.
- **Status.** A task's stored state is `todo`, `doing` or `done`. The `status` it reports is `blocked` when its state is `todo` and at least one of its dependencies is not `done` or one of its part lines is not fully allocated; otherwise its state.
  - `POST /tasks/{id}/start` with `{"on": date}`: only from `todo` with every dependency `done` and every part line fully allocated (409 otherwise); `on` is not before the project's `startDate` nor before any dependency's `finishedOn` + 1 day (400 otherwise). The task becomes `doing` with `startedOn` = `on`.
  - `POST /tasks/{id}/finish` with `{"on": date}`: only from `doing` (409 otherwise); `on` is not before `startedOn` (400 otherwise). The task becomes `done` with `finishedOn` = `on`, and its allocated part units leave the workshop, as when an order ships (its part lines keep their `allocated` counts as a record).
  - `POST /tasks/{id}/reopen`: only from `done` (409 otherwise). The task becomes `todo` and loses `startedOn` and `finishedOn` (both null). Its part lines then take units again, as if the task had just been added: `allocated` starts at 0, units come from `free`, and the rest is backordered behind every line already waiting. Every task that depends on it directly or through other tasks and is `doing` also becomes `todo` and loses `startedOn`; it keeps its allocated part units. Tasks that are `done` stay `done`.
  - All return 200 with the task.
- `DELETE /tasks/{id}` removes a task: 409 if it has dependents, unless `?cascade=true` is given, in which case its dependents are removed too, and theirs, and so on. The allocated part units of each removed task that is not `done` become available, task by task in the order added, part by part in the order given. 204.
- `GET /projects/{id}/schedule` returns 200 with `{"tasks": [{"id", "start", "finish", "slack", "critical", "missing"}, ...], "end"}`, computed this way:
  - A `done` task has `start` = `startedOn` and `finish` = `finishedOn`. A `doing` task has `start` = `startedOn` and `finish` = `startedOn` + `duration` - 1 day. A `todo` task (blocked or not) has `start` = the latest of the project's `startDate` and each dependency's `finish` + 1 day, and `finish` = `start` + `duration` - 1 day.
  - `end` is the latest `finish` of all tasks (`null` for a project without tasks).
  - Latest dates: a task without dependents has `latestFinish` = `end`; otherwise `latestFinish` = the earliest of each dependent's `latestStart` - 1 day; `latestStart` = `latestFinish` - `duration` + 1 day. For a `done` or `doing` task, `slack` is 0; for a `todo` task, `slack` = the number of days from `start` to `latestStart` (never negative; 0 when `latestStart` is not after `start`).
  - `critical` is true when `slack` is 0 and the task is not `done`.
  - `missing` is the number of units the task's part lines still miss (0 for a `done` task).
  - Tasks are listed by `start`, then by the order they were added.
- `GET /tasks/{id}` returns 200 with the task. `GET /projects/{id}/tasks?status=s` lists the project's tasks in the order added, optionally only those whose reported `status` is `s` (`todo`, `blocked`, `doing` or `done`; anything else is 400).

## The library

- **The workshop's date.** The server keeps its own current date, `today`, which starts at `2026-11-01`. `GET /clock` returns 200 with `{"today"}`. `POST /clock` with `{"today": date}` moves it forward (a date before the current one is 409; the same date is allowed and changes nothing); 200 with `{"today", "expired": [holdId, ...]}` (see holds). Every rule that says "today" uses this date.
- **Members are customers.** Every customer is a library member; `memberId` below is a customer's id. A customer is `{"id", "name", "creditLimit", "openValue", "fines", "loans": [loan, ...], "holds": [hold, ...]}`: `fines` is the unpaid total, `loans` their current loans in the order borrowed, `holds` their waiting and ready holds in the order placed. A new customer has 0 fines and no loans or holds.
- `POST /titles` with `{"name": string, "copies": integer}` registers a title (name 1 to 200 characters, copies 1 to 20). 201 with the title. A title is `{"id", "name", "copies", "onLoan", "ready", "free", "queue": [holdId, ...]}`: `onLoan` copies are lent out, `ready` copies are set aside for a hold, `free` = `copies - onLoan - ready`, and `queue` lists the title's waiting holds in queue order.
- A member is **blocked** when their `fines` are 500 or more.
- `POST /loans` with `{"memberId", "titleId"}` lends a copy. Refused with 409 when the member is blocked, already has 3 loans, or already has a loan of this title. If the member has a ready hold on the title, that hold is fulfilled: it is removed and its set-aside copy is lent. Otherwise a `free` copy is lent, but only when the title's queue is empty; with no free copy, or with members waiting in the queue, it is 409. 201 with the loan `{"id", "memberId", "titleId", "borrowedOn", "dueOn", "renewals", "returnedOn": null}`, `borrowedOn` = today, `dueOn` = today + 14 days, `renewals` = 0.
- `POST /loans/{id}/renew` adds 14 days to `dueOn` and 1 to `renewals`. Refused with 409 when the loan was returned, `renewals` is already 2, the loan is overdue (today is after `dueOn`), the member is blocked, or the title's queue is not empty. 200 with the loan.
- `POST /loans/{id}/return` returns the copy (409 if already returned). The fine is 20 yen for each day today is after `dueOn`, at most 600 yen for one loan; it is added to the member's `fines`. The copy then goes to the first waiting hold in the title's queue, which becomes ready (see holds); with nobody waiting it becomes free. 200 with `{"loan": loan with returnedOn = today, "fine"}`. A returned loan leaves the member's `loans`.
- `POST /holds` with `{"memberId", "titleId"}` places a hold. Refused with 409 when the member is blocked, already has 5 holds, already has a loan or a hold on this title, or when the title has a free copy and an empty queue (the member should borrow it instead). 201 with the hold `{"id", "memberId", "titleId", "status": "waiting", "placedOn", "readyUntil": null}`; it joins the end of the queue.
- **Ready holds.** When a copy goes to a waiting hold, the hold leaves the queue, its `status` becomes `ready`, `readyUntil` = today + 3 days, and the copy is counted in the title's `ready`. Only that hold's member can borrow the set-aside copy.
- **Expiry.** When the clock moves to a new date, every ready hold whose `readyUntil` is before the new today expires, in order of `readyUntil`, then of the time it became ready: its status becomes `expired`, it leaves the member's `holds`, and its copy goes to the next waiting hold (which becomes ready with `readyUntil` = the new today + 3) or becomes free. `expired` in the clock response lists the holds that expired, in that order.
- `DELETE /holds/{id}` cancels a waiting or ready hold (409 if it already expired or was fulfilled): it leaves the queue or, if it was ready, its copy goes to the next waiting hold as above. 204.
- `POST /customers/{id}/payments` with `{"amount"}` pays fines: 1 to the member's `fines` (409 if more). 200 with the customer. Paying below 500 unblocks the member; it does not change holds or loans.
- `GET /titles/{id}`, `GET /loans/{id}` and `GET /holds/{id}` return 200 with the object as defined above (a hold's `status` is one of `waiting`, `ready`, `expired`, `fulfilled`, `cancelled`).

## Shared expenses

- **Groups of customers.** Customers form groups that record, over time, what each paid on the others' behalf. A group's members are customers: `customerId` below is a customer's id (404 if unknown).
- `POST /groups` with `{"name": string, "startDate": date, "members": [{"customerId", "weight"?: integer}, ...]}` creates a group. There are 2 to 20 members, each customer at most once in the list (400 otherwise), `weight` is 1 to 100 and defaults to 1. Every member joins on `startDate`. The response is 201 with `{"id", "name", "startDate", "members": [member, ...]}`, members in the order given. A member is `{"id", "customerId", "joinedOn", "leftOn", "weights": [{"from": date, "weight"}, ...]}`; `leftOn` is null while the member has not left; `weights` is sorted by `from`. The group's member order is the order members were added (those of `POST /groups` first, in the order given).
- `POST /groups/{id}/members` with `{"customerId", "joinedOn": date, "weight"?}` adds a member (201, the member). `joinedOn` is on or after `startDate`; at most 20 members in all, including those who left; a customer who is or was a member of the group is 409.
- A member is **active on** a date d when `joinedOn <= d` and (`leftOn` is null or `d <= leftOn`). The member's **weight on** d is the `weight` of the last `weights` entry with `from <= d`.
- `PUT /groups/{id}/members/{memberId}/weight` with `{"weight", "from": date}` sets the member's weight from that date on (an entry with the same `from` is replaced); `from` is on or after `joinedOn`. 200 with the member.
- `POST /groups/{id}/members/{memberId}/leave` with `{"leftOn": date}` makes the member leave; they are active through `leftOn` itself. `leftOn` is on or after `joinedOn`. 409 if the member already left, or if an expense or refund dated after `leftOn` names them as payer or lists them in an `equal` or `shares` split. 200 with the member.
- `POST /groups/{id}/expenses` with `{"payerId", "amount"?, "orderId"?, "date", "split"}` records an expense; exactly one of `amount` and `orderId` is given (400 otherwise). `amount` is 1 to 10,000,000; `date` is on or after `startDate` and not after today (409 if after today). `split` is one of:
  - `{"all": true}`: the beneficiaries are the members active on `date`, each with their weight on `date` as share;
  - `{"equal": [memberId, ...]}`: share 1 each;
  - `{"shares": {memberId: integer 1 to 100, ...}}`.
  The payer and every listed beneficiary must be active on `date` (409 otherwise); a beneficiary may be the payer; a member may be listed only once. 201 with the expense `{"id", "payerId", "amount", "orderId", "date", "split", "owed": {memberId: yen, ...}, "refunded": 0}`; `orderId` is null when not given.
- **Orders as expenses.** With `orderId` (404 if unknown) the expense's `amount` is that order's `total`. The order must be `shipped`, its customer must be the payer's customer, and no other expense of any group may name it (409 otherwise).
- How `owed` is computed (for every split): each beneficiary owes `floor(amount * share / totalShares)`. The yen left over are given one each to the beneficiaries with the largest fractional parts of `amount * share / totalShares`; ties go to the beneficiary earlier in the member order. The `owed` values add up to `amount`.
- An `all` split is not fixed when recorded: whenever an expense is read or counted, its beneficiaries and shares are those of its date at that moment. Adding a member who joins on or before the date, changing a weight that applies on the date, or a `leftOn` before the date therefore changes an `all` expense's `owed`. Expenses that have refunds are the exception: their `owed` is frozen at the moment of their first refund.
- `PUT /groups/{id}/expenses/{expenseId}` with the same body replaces an expense (200, same id; the order it already names is allowed), `DELETE` removes it (204); both are 409 when the expense has a refund. Unknown expense: 404.
- `POST /groups/{id}/expenses/{expenseId}/refunds` with `{"amount", "date"}` records money the shop gave back to the expense's payer. `amount` is 1 to the expense's `amount` minus its earlier refunds (409 if more); `date` is on or after the expense's date (400 otherwise) and not after today (409 if after today). An expense with an `orderId` takes refunds only from returns: this request is 409 for it. Each beneficiary's `reduction` is computed with the same largest-remainder rule as `owed`, using the expense's frozen `owed` values as shares and the refund amount as amount. 201 with `{"id", "expenseId", "amount", "date", "reduction": {memberId: yen, ...}}`. The expense's `refunded` is the sum of its refunds.
- **Returns refund the expense.** When units of an order named by an expense are returned (`POST /orders/{id}/returns`), the app also records a refund of that expense with `amount` = the returned quantity times the line's `unitPrice` and `date` = today, computed as above, also when the payer or a beneficiary has left.
- `GET /groups/{id}/balances?asOf=date` returns 200 with `[{"memberId", "balance"}, ...]`, every member in member order (including those who left). Counted are the expenses and refunds dated on or before `asOf` (all of them when `asOf` is omitted). An expense adds `amount` to its payer and subtracts each beneficiary's `owed`; a refund subtracts `amount` from the expense's payer and adds each beneficiary's `reduction`. The balances add up to 0.
- `GET /groups/{id}/settlement` returns 200 with `[{"from", "to", "amount"}, ...]` over all expenses and refunds: repeat while any balance is not 0: the member with the most negative balance (ties: earlier in member order) pays the member with the largest positive balance (ties: earlier in member order) the smaller of the two absolute amounts. List the transfers in the order made; all balances 0 gives `[]`.
- `GET /groups/{id}/expenses?member=memberId&from=date&to=date` lists expenses (all query parameters optional; `member` matches the payer or a beneficiary as of now; `from` / `to` are inclusive) in date order, ties in the order recorded, each with its current `owed` and `refunded`.

## Errors

- Invalid JSON, a missing or wrong-typed field, or a value outside the rules above is 400; an unknown product, customer, order, project, task, title, loan, hold, group, member or expense is 404; a duplicate sku and the refusals named above are 409. A refused request changes nothing. Every error body is `{"error": string}`.
- Data is kept in memory; it does not have to survive a restart.
